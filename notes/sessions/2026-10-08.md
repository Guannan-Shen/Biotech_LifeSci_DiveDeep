# Session 2026-10-08: Business quality gate (product, moat, business model, people)

## Goal
Investor review of the HROW and MRLN memos: the analysis of products and services was missing.
Evaluate the major products and revenue sources, whether they carry a competitive advantage,
whether the business model is normal, good or great, and whether the team can deliver the plan,
including a willingness to sacrifice the short term for the long term. For HROW, VEVYE and
IHEEZO matter most. Check whether the framework already covered this, plan, execute.

## Blueprint check before executing
The framework did not cover it. Its three gates (evidence, survival, price) never asked what a
company sells, whether that is defensible, or whether management delivers; the archetype table
mentioned "capital allocation" once. The HROW memo mentioned VEVYE once and IHEEZO not at all.
This changes the design, so the blueprint (v0.4), the decision log (D-030 to D-033) and the
framework were updated before any code. Direction re-checked after drafting: the gate serves the
XBI mandate through left-tail avoidance (licensor, payer and reimbursement fragility are left-tail
sources) and through better scenario weights; it adds a testable hypothesis (H15) instead of a
decorative score.

## Data access today
WebFetch failed for harrow.com and fool.com (DNS), as for sec.gov before. Web search worked and
supplied product revenue, deal terms, patents, guidance history and the MRLN program status. All
filing-derived numbers are `unverified` until the linked 8-K, 10-Q and 10-K are read.

## Findings worth remembering
- HROW is two franchises on one chassis (ocular surface: VEVYE, TYRVAYA, BYQLOVI, compounding;
  retina: IHEEZO, biosimilars, TRIESENCE). VEVYE has a narrow, stable moat and failed the price
  test in H1; IHEEZO is a reimbursement product whose moat is eroding (pass-through ended
  2026-04-01; 2025 revenue inflated by channel loading). TRIESENCE is the best moat per dollar.
- HROW business model: good at the bottom edge (9/16, pricing power 0); deal sourcing is the real
  capability (VEVYE about 11x 2025 revenue per dollar paid; TYRVAYA bought for USD 30M upfront).
- HROW people: 2 of 7 guides hit, posterior 0.36 (0.19-0.55), signed error -4.3%: discount the
  guidance, not the strategy. The 2026 sales build is the one pre-committed sacrifice; checked in
  March 2027.
- H2 bridge (A): no plausible VEVYE x IHEEZO cell reaches the USD 350M low end; re-weighting the
  memo's scenarios under the credibility cap moves the value from about 32 to about 29 vs 30.35.
- MRLN: model unproven (revenue about 7% of cost base), moat none to narrow and eroding on the
  civil side after the NZ withdrawal, people unknown; the target model would be great if
  certification arrives.
- TYRVAYA acquisition (closed H2 2026) and the OPUVIZ January 2027 US launch window were new to
  the repo.

# Session 2026-10-08 (second): Takeout database and Phase 2 gate

## Goal
Investor request: Viatris agreed to buy Pacira (USD 36.50 cash, about USD 1.65B). Build a database
of biotech takeouts, find the common traits, and look for the next target before the news. Also
test the view that clean, best-in-class Phase 2 data is where the odds turn (Phase 1 is safety,
most drugs fail Phase 2, clearing Phase 2 shifts the odds).

## Blueprint check before executing
Fits H8 (takeout likelihood) and M9 P5 and P6. It changes the design in two ways, so the blueprint
went to v0.5 and D-034 to D-037 were logged first: H8 is restated as a hazard model with a full
denominator (a target-only table cannot forecast), and a new H16 (Phase 2 quality drift) is added
and pre-registered. Direction re-checked after drafting: the takeout score is a tie-breaker inside
gated names (D-036), which keeps the XBI mandate and left-tail avoidance first.

## Data access today
Web search worked; WebFetch failed for sec.gov and biobucks.co (DNS). FMP M&A endpoints are
plan-blocked. Deal terms came from search summaries of filings and press releases, so all rows are
`unverified`.

## Done
- Data: `data/reference/biotech_takeouts.csv` (88 deals, 24 sourced 2025-2026 rows with premiums
  where given; 64 recall rows flagged).
- Code: `models/takeout.py` (deal loader and summary, odds-ratio takeout score, kicker, basket
  Poisson-binomial, Beta hazard from counts), `models/phase2.py` (normal helpers, shrinkage,
  assurance, Gelman-Carlin exaggeration ratio, scorecard), `config/takeout_priors.yaml`,
  `scripts/takeout_screen.py`; 12 new tests, 75 pass, ruff clean.
- Docs: `docs/research/2026-10-08_takeout_database_and_phase2_gate.md`; H16 pre-registration;
  blueprint v0.5 (H8 restated, H16 added); M9 doc; correction to the 10-07 launch-layer premiums.

## Findings worth remembering
- 65% of targets had randomized Phase 2, pivotal or commercial data before the deal; 35% were
  approved; 80% of buyers were big pharma; median equity value about USD 3.2B.
- Median premium to the last close 40% (IQR 28% to 63%, n=14 sourced deals); run-up before
  announcement shows in the VWAP gap (Ventyx 2% vs 62%).
- Base hazard about 3.5% a year (A) makes the takeout a kicker of about 1-5% a year per name;
  a basket of 15 names at 8% gives a 71% chance of at least one deal a year.
- A z = 2.5 Phase 2 implies about 54% Phase 3 assurance under a modest prior, close to the
  published 58% Phase 3 transition rate.
- Pacira: activist proxy contest (DOMA) plus a dated generic entry (2030) plus a generics-major
  buyer; the analog for commercial_pharma names.
